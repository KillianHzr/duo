# Privacy policy

Peinard is a voice chat app for people on the same local network. It is built so that your
conversations and your data stay on your own computers.

## What Peinard does not do

- It has no account, no sign-up and no advertising.
- It collects no personal data, no usage statistics and no crash reports.
- It never sends your voice, your name or your settings to the developer or to any third party.
- It never records your voice conversations.

## What stays on your network

- **Your voice** travels directly between the computers in the call, over your local network,
  encrypted by WebRTC. It does not go through the Internet or through any server.
- **Your first name and your mute status** are sent to the computer hosting the call, on your
  local network, so the other people in the call can see who is there.
- **The access code** you type is sent to the hosting computer to let you in.
- **Chat messages, the files you send and shared notes** are sent to the hosting computer, on your
  local network, and kept there in a folder its owner chooses. People in the call can read them.
  If the host turns on the daily backup, a copy goes to the folder the host picked; where that
  folder is synced to (a cloud drive, for example) is the host's choice.

The hosting computer only accepts connections from local-network addresses.

## What is stored on your computer

Your first name, your audio settings, the look you picked (colour, light or dark), whether you host or
join, and the access code. They are kept
in the app's data folder on your computer and are removed when you uninstall Peinard.

## Connections to the Internet

- **Installer downloaded from GitHub:** the app asks GitHub whether a newer version exists, and
  downloads it if so. GitHub receives your IP address, as with any download, under
  [GitHub's privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- **Microsoft Store version:** updates are handled by the Microsoft Store.
- **Link previews:** when a chat message contains a web address, the app shows that page's title,
  short description and picture under the message. To get them, your computer asks that website
  for its page and picture, once the message comes into view, as a browser would when opening the link.
  The website receives your IP address and the request; nothing else is sent (no cookies, no name,
  no message text). Addresses on your local network are never contacted this way.

Peinard is not designed to make any other connection to the Internet.

## Microphone

Peinard uses your microphone only while the app is connected to a call, and only to send your voice to
the other people in that call.

## Push to talk

If you turn on push to talk, Peinard watches the one key or mouse button you chose, system-wide, to know
when you hold it. It does not record or send what you type. With push to talk off, Peinard does not
watch the keyboard or the mouse.
